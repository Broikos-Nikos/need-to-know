SECTION 1  Ways of working
Audience: Human Resources
Classification: Internal

Core working arrangements set the expectations for how teams communicate, share calendars and respond to requests, and every manager is responsible for making these expectations clear within the team. Team members are expected to keep their calendars up to date so that colleagues can see availability without asking. Requests for a meeting should state the purpose and the expected outcome, and they should respect the time that others need to prepare. Urgent matters are raised by telephone rather than by a message that may sit unread. Managers set the response expectations for their team and review them when the workload changes. These arrangements are reviewed by the human resources team with input from staff, and any change is communicated before it takes effect. Managers are asked to explain new expectations at the next team meeting, so that everyone hears the same message at the same time.

SECTION 2  Training
Audience: Human Resources
Classification: Internal

Training is planned from the skills each team needs over the coming planning period, and the human resources team works with managers to set priorities before budgets are committed. Mandatory courses on conduct, data protection and safety are scheduled for all staff, and the completion record is kept in the learning system. Optional courses are available on request, and the manager decides whether attendance fits the workload and the development plan. Trainers who work for the company are selected through a standard process that checks their qualifications and their experience with corporate clients. Results from course evaluations are shared with the manager and are used to improve the content, not to rank the people who attended. Managers who want more support for a particular course can raise it in the planning discussion, where priorities are set for the whole business.

SECTION 3  Ways of working, part 2
Audience: Human Resources
Classification: Internal

Flexible arrangements, such as adjusted start times or compressed working patterns, are approved by the line manager against the needs of the team, and the approval is recorded in the employee file. The manager considers whether the team can cover calls, deadlines and customer contact during the flexible period before agreeing, and the decision is communicated in writing. A flexible arrangement can be reviewed when the team's needs change, and the employee is told the reason for any change in good time. Requests that are declined are explained with the business reasons, and the employee can ask for the decision to be reviewed by the human resources team. Equal treatment applies, so that the same type of request is judged in the same way across teams.

SECTION 4  Training, part 2
Audience: Human Resources
Classification: Internal

Managers receive development training when they are appointed and again at later points in their careers, so that the skills of coaching, feedback and performance conversations stay current. Each programme is designed with a clear objective and an assessment that shows whether the manager can apply the skill in a real situation. Managers who complete the programme agree a development plan with their own line manager, and the plan is reviewed during the normal performance cycle. The human resources team tracks completion rates across the business and reports any gaps to senior leaders. Training budgets for managers are controlled centrally, so that no team is left without development because of its local budget. Managers who have completed the programme are invited to mentor new managers, which keeps the learning alive within the business.

SECTION 5  Ways of working, part 3
Audience: Human Resources
Classification: Internal

Remote working is a privilege that depends on the job, the team and the employee's performance, and this policy sets out the rules that apply to office staff who want to work away from the building. Office staff may work remotely up to 3 days a week. Remote days are agreed with the line manager and recorded in the shared calendar, so that the team knows where each person will be. Employees must have a suitable workspace, a secure connection and the equipment supplied by the company, and they must follow the information security rules whenever they work away from the office. The manager can ask for changes to the pattern when the team needs in-person presence, and the employee is given reasonable notice of any such change.
